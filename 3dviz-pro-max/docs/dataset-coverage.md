# Dataset coverage and remaining depth

The [generated catalog](../skills/3dviz-pro-max/data/catalog-summary.json) is the count source of truth. A populated direction means it has authored recipes; it does not mean all subjects within it are covered or rendered.

## How the dataset is organized

Recipes describe a complete visualization task. Reusable knowledge describes a decision that can transfer between tasks. Sources support bounded claims. Public evidence records authorship, source checks and actual artifact observations separately.

The physical storage uses manifest layout version 2: each `recipe.<slug>` is one object at `data/recipes/<slug>.json`, and each `knowledge.<slug>` is one object at `data/knowledge/<exact-knowledge-kind>/<slug>.json`. Sources and directions remain arrays. Recipe paths do not encode a primary direction; `direction_ids` is unordered membership used to generate every applicable direction view. The original planning categories remain useful content axes; they do not require a separate loader or skill for each category.

| Content axis | Current representation | Remaining depth to assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Topic directions | Direction registry, unordered recipe membership and generated direction views | Breadth within each direction, advanced and mixed-domain cases |
| Styles and themes | 37 style profiles (33 with numeric defaults, five of them named looks) and 18 theme profiles in separate knowledge families | Useful combinations, object-specific treatment and culturally grounded historical claims when needed |
| Representation | 12 geometry profiles, recipe geometry and inspection patterns | Broader subject-specific use and observed geometry checks |
| Object construction | Object archetypes and recipe object craft | More detailed object families, articulation and functional assemblies |
| Materials and lighting | Nineteen material profiles, 22 lighting profiles (eight of them mood rigs with numeric defaults) and recipe guidance | Observed optical conditions, encoding and controlled comparisons |
| Motion and interactions | State contracts, 19 physical behaviors, 12 motion profiles and 11 interaction profiles and inspection patterns | Observed input, accessibility and solver handoffs |
| Composition and labels | Eight presentation profiles, eight composition profiles and recipe guidance | Observed framing, labels and responsive behavior in concrete artifacts |
| Tool and solver integration | Twenty-eight tool adapters, physical contracts and 12 output profiles | Executed import/conversion paths, richer source formats and host integration |
| Reasoning and validation | 99 conditional reasoning rules, 75 domain-validation records, profile records and recipe invariants | Observed application of scoped recommendations and domain checks |
| Reusable geometry | 22 kit blueprints, each a record plus a module under `templates/kits/` plus four captures under `evidence/kits/` | More subjects beyond a village, and a second look family the kits were tuned against |

The blueprint family is the one place where the dataset ships runnable geometry rather than guidance about geometry. Each of the 22 records names an asset under `templates/kits/`, its tunable `params` with the range that was proved, its named `sockets`, a three-band `detail_ladder`, a `footprint_m`, a `poly_budget` and the `fits_looks` ids that `design-context.py --blueprints --look <id>` filters on. Coverage is a village at a diorama scale: six buildings, eight props, four plants and rocks, two walking creatures and two Blender-authored GLB heroes, proved at 1280x720 with ACESFilmic tone mapping at exposure 1.0 and no post-processing. What that does not cover: vehicles, interiors, machinery, any non-European vernacular, and any look outside the hand-painted and stylized families the palettes were tuned in. The captures record what one headless browser drew — they are not evidence that a blueprint looks good, and nothing in the family certifies historical construction, botanical accuracy or structural behaviour. Nine primitives and `layout/village-layout.js` ship as modules with no record, because neither has a capture of its own to point at. Every one of the 22 records declares a `tiers` block, and each tier is either proved by its own captures or says why not. All 22 declare T0, the blockout. The procedural **T2** tier is proved on **18**; the four that are `not-proved` state a measured reason — `quadruped-walker` and `tree-conifer` because a fabric weave on fur and a bark map on needles read worse than T1, `stone-guildhall` and `iron-lantern` because they are glTF records whose geometry never reaches `buildKit`. **T3** is proved on **2** (`timber-cottage`, `watermill`) and declared `missing` on the other 20. **T4** is `missing` on all 22.

Quality has a stated ceiling. The procedural T2 tier is stylized-game quality: canvas-drawn textures and vertex-baked occlusion, built in the browser with no shipped files. T3 adds what a runtime cannot compute — bevelled arrises, a UV atlas and light baked into textures — and the two heroes above ship at that tier, baked in Blender from the modules' own geometry. Film or photoreal work needs authored T4 assets with hand-built topology and edge flow, and **none ship here**: T4 is a schema slot every record declares missing. A tier a record does not prove is a tier it does not claim.

Inline invariants are not automatically counted as standalone validation records. Source count is not a quality score. A recipe with a numerical formula still needs an implemented solver or explicit analytic evaluation before it becomes a simulation.

## Numeric coverage and provenance

Records that used to describe a look in adjectives now carry numbers a scene can be built from. 33 style profiles, eight mood lighting profiles and three camera / time-of-day records (46 knowledge records in total) hold an authored `defaults` block stating palette, sky, fog, light rig, tone mapping and exposure, camera, materials, motion and post in one comparable shape; five of the style profiles are named looks written as complete art directions. 21 recipes hold numeric `defaults` with a worked expected value: 18 recipes split evenly across anatomy, physiology and molecules, plus three corrected mechanics, astronomy and robotics recipes, alongside three state tables (cardiac cycle, lever classes, slip systems). Where a record declares no tolerance of its own, `knowledge.validation-default-tolerance-rule` supplies the default. Four factual tooling records cover glTF punctual light units, the Blender view-transform to tone-mapping table, headless glTF export and the glTF material extension matrix.

The `defaults` block states its own provenance, and the distinction matters more than the numbers. **`source-backed`** defaults cite claim IDs read from a registered source — 13 of the 21 recipe blocks are source-backed. **`authored`** defaults are editorial starting points tuned against a stated exposure and mood: all 46 knowledge blocks and the remaining eight recipe blocks are authored, because their sources print no numbers. No renderer, solver or measuring instrument produced any authored value. Every block also carries `known_limits`, and any of them may be changed when a brief asks for something else.

Their expected outcomes are review criteria, not recorded passes.
