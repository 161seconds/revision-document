# Visual review prompts

These are conditional prompts for judgment, not a universal scoring system. Apply the ones relevant
to the subject, visual direction and delivery. A blank background, centered camera, still scene,
limited material palette or mathematically regular grid can be exactly right. Routine inapplicable
items need no opt-out document. Preserve historical scorecards as observations under their old rubric.

## Shape and identity

1. **Does the important object read correctly?** Check silhouette, proportions, negative space,
   distinctive landmarks and part relationships before adding decoration. A generic proxy with
   elaborate texture is still a generic proxy.
2. **Does construction fit the object?** Look for thickness, attachments, cavities, joints and
   continuity where the subject needs them. Reconsider the kit or modeling method if those are wrong.
3. **Is detail purposeful at the actual viewing scale?** Inspect overview and close-up. Organisms,
   machines and buildings need different features; mathematical geometry may need precision and
   clean boundaries rather than wear or texture. The kit detail ladder is a scoped example, not a
   requirement for two features per band or a universal three-metre viewing distance.

## Material and light

4. **Do materials have the intended identity?** Inspect response to the actual light: soft tissue,
   satin, metal, stone, glass, diagrammatic color or another deliberate abstraction. Roughness
   variation is useful when the object calls for it; no minimum material or roughness count applies.
5. **Does surface treatment support form?** Prefer appropriate scale and direction of detail. Noise,
   bevels, bake resolution, PBR labels and polygon count do not establish craftsmanship by themselves.
6. **Does lighting reveal what matters?** Check clipped highlights, crushed shadows, excessive fill,
   muddy reflection and readable boundaries. Each light should have a purpose. Studio, flat graphic,
   emissive and environmental approaches are all available; one light is not automatically wrong.
7. **Are effects earning their cost?** Fog, bloom, depth of field, transparency and reflection can
   help, but none is mandatory. Avoid effects that hide source structure, shift perceived boundaries
   or turn a non-emissive object into an apparent light source unless that is intentional art.

## Composition and communication

8. **Does the composition guide attention?** Focal contrast, scale, overlap, rhythm, whitespace and
   color can establish hierarchy. Do not require the hero to be the brightest area or constrain every
   palette to a fixed hue count. Compare groups equally when comparison is the purpose.
9. **Is depth useful and legible?** An environment may benefit from atmospheric layers; an isolated
   organ, diagram or sculpture may benefit from an empty background. Do not add scenery for a check.
10. **Is scale interpreted correctly?** Use units, a scale bar, familiar references or explicit
    enlargement when relevant. Doors and human figures are not universal scale cues. Separate
    physical/model units from display exaggeration.
11. **Does the camera fit this representation?** Perspective and orthographic projections are both
    valid. Choose framing, lens and controls for the subject; inspect clipping and UI occlusion at
    the actual viewport size. Symmetry and a centered view may communicate best.
12. **Are repetition and variation intentional?** Instance repeated geometry when appropriate.
    Jitter helps some natural scenes; it can falsify crystal lattices, anatomy, gears or exact grids.

## Behavior and truth

13. **Does expected motion happen?** Verify a relevant transition, not arbitrary pixel differences.
    A resting scene or completed animation may correctly stop rendering. Stillness can be art.
14. **Do objects remain coherently related?** Check supports, joints, swept clearance, collision,
    occlusion and parent-child attachment during motion. Review the context around the hero too.
15. **Do controls and reset tell the truth?** Parameters must change the represented state and give
    useful feedback. Visibility controls must not silently change physical parameters. Reset should
    restore the stated defaults or clearly preserve chosen settings.
16. **Do scientific visuals preserve the researched claim?** Verify coordinates, units, topology,
    equations, orientation, labels and limits. Source geometry does not verify authored deformation;
    a smooth animation does not verify a solver. Do not invent missing anatomy to fill the screen.
17. **Does the finished view serve the brief?** After correctness checks, judge the artwork itself.
    Consider whether a different silhouette, material, camera or construction route would improve
    it. Passing tools or this list cannot establish that the art direction is good.

## When an explanation feels unclear

Use [subject clarity and meaningful motion](../../references/subject-clarity-and-motion.md): can the
viewer identify the subject, interpret its colors, and connect a control to what changes and what
stays fixed? Check labels after filtering and sectioning, local attachment/clearance during
deformation, and the resting and extreme states that matter. More motion or detail is not a remedy
for a mismatched model, unreadable material or ambiguous explanation.
